# USB input PR #468: buffer placement, latency, and test evidence

27 September 2026. This note reviews [Bryan Tysinger's draft PR #468](https://github.com/sambanks/octabam/pull/468), head `f1432c9d83b4596ba6c6274d5434a18ee7355c25`, against Octatrack OS 1.40C. It records what I checked locally and what remains for a MKI test. The USB input implementation belongs to Bryan's `usb-io` remix; it has not been integrated into Octalab.

## Evidence and limits

| Level | Observation |
|---|---|

Under a busy project, bad USB OUT packet completions went from 1,189/min with stock crossbar settings to 5–11/min with SCM bursting, then zero over ten minutes after assigning USB first priority on the SDRAM and SRAM crossbar slaves. The operator also noticed the A/B audio-record LEDs glowing orange around the test. The first duplex probe sent separate 440/660/880/1100 Hz tones at 0.05 full scale on host channels 1–4; the PR maps those channels to OT inputs A–D. The stock record LEDs can display external input level, which makes this a useful clue that USB audio reached at least the input metering path. The observation has no exact timestamp, and record-setup source selection can also affect these LEDs. It does **not** yet prove all four DSP input channels, their physical labels, or uninterrupted audio. The later input-only capture sent no tone from the host.

After the operator confirmed that the unit was responsive and disconnected the analog A/B cables, a bounded output-only probe sent a 440 Hz sine at 0.05 full scale on **host channel 1 only**, with channels 2–4 zero. The buffer contained three seconds of audio; CoreAudio took 4.79 seconds to finish. OUT received 13,590 more packets, including 11,226 additional nonzero packets, with no `bad`, OUT underrun, or OUT re-prime. Meanwhile USB IN overruns rose **20,690 → 24,995 (+4,305)** and IN self-heal re-primes **0 → 45**. With the client closed, the overrun and packet counters stopped increasing for another three seconds; USB control requests still responded. The operator reported **LED A lit during the channel-1 probe** with the analog A/B cables disconnected. This supports channel 1 reaching the expected input metering path; it does not establish the recorder signal, uninterrupted audio, or channels 2–4. The test stopped before probing those channels. The `reprimes` rise adds a concrete EP3 IN queue symptom to investigate; it does not alone identify the initiating fault.

## macOS host failures: CoreAudio repeatedly stops the OT stream

These are observations on the same `OCTABAM94` image, with macOS 15.6.1 and the device enumerated as four inputs/four outputs at 44.1 kHz. They are **not** evidence that continuous recording works. The operator found that Ableton Live became severely unresponsive when the OT was selected for output; selecting it as input alone appeared usable. Arturia Analog Lab V standalone produced massive output dropouts. Audio Hijack and Finder Quick Look reproduced the failure with different clients. The OT's A/B input LEDs sometimes lit, but they do not prove an uninterrupted audio path.

The macOS SDK defines `0xE00002D7` as `kIOReturnOffline` (“device offline”). It is an error returned to CoreAudio's input read; it does **not**, by itself, establish a physical USB disconnection. The `IOWorkLoopDeinit ... stopping with error 0` entries count context shutdowns, not USB transfer errors. The repeated permission requests explain the flashing microphone indicator in duplex sessions. Finder's **output-only** failure shows that neither an intentional audio feedback loop nor repeatedly opening an input client is required to produce the choppy sound. The single Quick Look play-state interval also makes application-level start/stop requests alone an insufficient explanation for its 311 CoreAudio context restarts. The logs do not identify why those contexts stop.

Audio MIDI Setup 3.6 also crashed at 01:45:45.976. Its crash report and the unified log show an uncaught `NSInternalInconsistencyException`, `Invalid parameter not satisfying: aString != nil`, on the main thread in AppKit `NSCell setStringValue:` / `NSTextField setStringValue:`. This is the immediate UI crash; neither the report nor the available logs identify the missing value or prove that firmware caused it. No Audio Hijack hang/crash report was found for the frozen elapsed-time display, so a UI-thread deadlock is unproven.

### Working hypotheses and review priorities

1. **EP3 IN continuity and implicit feedback — strongest current lead, not proven.** The PR declares asynchronous EP3 OUT and marks EP3 IN as its implicit-feedback data source (`bmAttributes 0x25`). The earlier **output-only** firmware counters already showed +4,305 USB IN ring overruns and +45 IN queue re-primes in a three-second requested tone, while OUT packet errors and underruns stayed at zero. An unstable IN stream could disturb host pacing of OUT, including when the application asks only for output. Correlate EP3 IN packet/dTD continuity, actual alternate-setting changes, the OUT ring and CoreAudio context stops before attributing causality.
2. **USB Audio control/descriptors — concrete defects to fix, cause of this failure unproven.** `GET_INTERFACE(5)` reports alt 0 after alt 1 is selected, `SET_INTERFACE(5)` accepts every nonzero alt as 1, and full-speed OUT is advertised but not implemented. Audit these and the clock/implicit-feedback descriptors against UAC2, particularly the behavior when the host opens output only. Basic macOS enumeration does not establish full streaming compliance.
3. **Endpoint queue and crossbar timing.** Bryan's crossbar change eliminated bad OUT packets on his unit, and our OUT counters were clean, but our EP3 IN overrun/re-prime evidence remains. Check IN queue ownership, completion/prime sequencing and bus service under the four-in/four-out profile. Moving the OUT packet buffers back to SDRAM or lowering latency cushions would not address the observed CoreAudio restarts on its own.
4. **A quantitative clue across the runs: the stream advances at roughly half speed. HYPOTHESIS, from the numbers above only.** Every host run took about twice its audio length: 12 s of requested audio took ~23 s (ratio 0.52); the input-only capture delivered 2.69 s of frames in 5.59 s (0.48); the 3 s tone took 4.79 s (0.63). The CoreAudio context stops fall on whole multiples of the 512-frame host buffer (1,536, 2,048, 3,072, 4,608 frames), i.e. at host IO-cycle boundaries, not mid-buffer. The simplest reading is that **EP3 IN, the implicit-feedback source, completes about half the samples its descriptor promises** (11.025 frames per 250 µs at `bInterval 2`). The device clock seen by CoreAudio would then run near half rate; the HAL would reject the timeline and restart IO, which is the output-only choppiness, and in duplex its input read would fail (`kIOReturnOffline`). The same reading fits the firmware counters: the OT produces 44.1 k frames/s into the IN ring but the endpoint drains about half, hence the IN overruns and re-primes, while OUT stays clean because the host sizes OUT packets from IN. The overrun counter's unit has not been reconciled with the missing-frame count, so this is not yet a measurement. **Cheapest discriminating check, no new build:** with one output-only client open for ten seconds, sum the byte lengths of completed EP3 IN transfers (or count IN completions and zero-length/short packets) and compare with 44,100 × 4 channels × sample size per second; a result near 50 % would localise the fault to IN packet scheduling or sizing (for example one microframe in two served, or short packets). The `GET_INTERFACE(5)` defect remains a secondary suspect because it can make the host driver resynchronise.

The device has already matched macOS's built-in `AppleUSBAudio` class driver; a firmware or interface **signature** is not the missing step. Apple [recommends its built-in class driver](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3190-usb-audio-device-design-considerations) for conforming USB audio devices. These observations point to streaming behavior and protocol correctness. Until a simple output session remains open and advances continuously, the existing `OUT_TARGET`, `AUD_BAND` and `AUD_TARGET` values should not be tuned for latency.

For a targeted host-log reproduction, record the local test window and run `/usr/bin/log show --style compact --info --start 'YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS' --end 'YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS' --predicate 'process == "coreaudiod" OR process == "Audio Hijack" OR process == "QuickLookUIService"'`. Compare `IOWorkLoopInit`, `IOWorkLoopDeinit`, `IO Stopped Context ... after ... frames`, `Audio IO Overload`, `EndReading` and client `setPlayState` lines. Process IDs and times above apply only to these runs. Raw host logs and the personal crash report are not published.

## Where the latency comes from

`OUT_TARGET=384` makes the host-to-OT ring prefill by at least 384 frames before it plays. At 44.1 kHz that is **8.71 ms**. The ring capacity of 1,024 frames (23.22 ms) is a bound on storage, not its normal delay. The 16-frame DSP block is 0.36 ms; USB packet scheduling, the host audio stack and the rest of the OT signal path add more. No end-to-end latency has been measured here.

The outgoing OT-to-host path has `AUD_TARGET=512` (11.61 ms) and a servo deadband `AUD_BAND=128` (±2.90 ms). The host uses that outgoing stream as implicit feedback for its incoming packets. Consequently its fill variation can propagate into the OUT ring. `AUD_TARGET` is directly a delay in the **OT-to-host** direction; treating it as another fixed 11.61 ms added to the **host-to-OT** direction would overstate the latter. Reducing it may help round-trip monitoring and change the incoming stream's dynamics, but it needs a separate measurement.

Bryan's suggested targets have these nominal OUT-ring cushions: 128 frames = 2.90 ms; 64 frames = 1.45 ms. The source comments estimate that the current ±128-frame servo band can let the OUT fill swing over roughly 256 frames. A 64- or 128-frame target with that band may underrun even with zero bad USB packets. The `minfill`/`maxfill` counters are sampled while consuming, after prefill; an underrun resets prefill. Read them together with `underruns`, `overruns`, `bad`, `err`, `partial`, `dry`, `late`, and the USB IN counters, over a sustained host stream. On our first MKI test, OUT `minfill` was 381 frames and `maxfill` 444 at the end, but the OUT ring had restarted and USB IN was losing time; those numbers are **not a safe basis for lowering `OUT_TARGET`**. An idle emulator run alone cannot set a safe target for a busy MKI.

## Proposed sequence for latency work

1. **Resolve the current stream loss before latency work:** reproduce the native five-second input-only capture with timestamps for every CoreAudio callback, device/USB logs, and simultaneous IN/OUT counter snapshots. Compare another host or host controller if available. Determine why only 2.69 s of input frames arrive and whether macOS repeatedly switches streaming alternate settings. Keep the project disposable and stop if the unit becomes unresponsive. Once stable, keep both audio directions open, route a distinct signal to each A–D input, record counters for at least ten minutes idle and under a busy project, then measure a host-output → OT-DIR/MAIN → host-input impulse loop. Record the host interface, buffer size and sample rate. Repeat after stopping and reopening the stream, a project load, CF writes, and a USB disconnect. Note whether physical A–D jacks return when the stream closes.
2. **Change `OUT_TARGET` alone**, using the lowest sustained `minfill` and a margin for unobserved host and CF stalls. Test 256, then 128 only if the measured margin supports it. Compare the same impulse route and counters. The existing PR data are insufficient to recommend 64 frames.
3. **Tighten `AUD_BAND` separately**, checking for servo hunting, alternating packet sizes, USB IN underruns/overruns, and OUT fill excursions. Only then consider lowering `AUD_TARGET`; that target mainly reduces OT-to-host latency and protection against producer stalls. Use one parameter change per build so a regression has a cause.

The packet-error fix and latency tuning should remain separate experiments. A zero `bad` count does not prove zero ring underruns or low round-trip latency.

## Open PR issues before broad use

- **DISK MODE:** Bryan reports a failure on an earlier image; the current head has not been retested. Check transition into and out of DISK MODE after a streaming session on a disposable card/project.
- **USB state:** full-speed OUT is advertised but not served. `GET_INTERFACE(5)` reports alt 0 while alt 1 streams. `SET_INTERFACE(5)` also accepts any nonzero alt as 1. These need specification-correct handling or an explicit supported-speed limit.
- **Global bus priority:** `out_up` enables SCM bursting and gives USB first priority on two crossbar slaves; `out_down` does not restore the stock settings. The packet result is compelling, but CAPTURE/CF-write and UI responsiveness under this priority have not been measured on our MKI.
- **SRAM claim:** the top 1 KiB was checked statically and under Bryan's port workloads. Recheck it during DISK MODE, CF activity and the configurations intended to be supported on hardware.
- **Diagnostic vendor requests:** the draft exposes peripheral reads and allowlisted register writes over USB. They are useful for experiments; shipping code should remove or tightly gate them.
- **Integration:** this `usb-io` build has no Octalab or CAPTURE. Four-channel recording during CAPTURE requires a later combined image and its own stress test.

No firmware image or stock OS bytes are published with this note.
